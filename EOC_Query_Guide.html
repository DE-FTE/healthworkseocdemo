EOC & Dental Playground — Query Template & Best Practices Guide
Reference Version: 1.0  |  HealthWorksAI  |  For Analysts & Plan Teams

🎯 Purpose

This guide gives you 10 ready-to-use template prompts and the best-practice principles behind them. Understanding why each template is written the way it is will help you write your own queries that get accurate, complete responses from the EOC & Dental Playground chatbot.

How to use this guide: Start with the template prompts as-is, then adapt them by swapping benefit names or adding specific fields. The annotations explain the design patterns — apply those same patterns to any benefit category.

🔬 Anatomy of a Great Query

Every high-accuracy query typically combines three elements:

ElementWhat it doesExample
Scope anchor Tells the AI which plans to search. Without this, the AI may guess or use a stale scope from a prior message. "for the selected plans", "for the plans in scope", "across the plans in scope"
Specific benefit & field Names the exact benefit category and, where possible, the specific data point. Vague topics produce vague answers. "dental exclusions", "preventive dental coverage amount", "OTC allowance and wallet type"
Output format Tells the AI how to present results. Without this, responses may be long prose instead of a structured, scannable layout. "in a table", "side-by-side tabular format", "as a comparison table with plans as columns"
Rule of thumb: If your query has all three elements above, you will almost always get a well-structured, accurate response. Missing even one (especially the scope anchor) is the most common cause of incomplete or off-target answers.

📝 10 Template Prompts with Annotations

Each prompt below is ready to use. The annotations explain the design pattern so you can build variations.

1
Transportation Comparison Scoped Table
Compare transportation benefits for the selected plans and show the differences in a table.
Why it works: "selected plans" anchors scope; "compare" and "show the differences" signals the AI to produce a side-by-side structure; "in a table" controls the output format.
Key fields extracted: Trip limits, mileage limits, transport type, rideshare allowed, copay, scheduling/cancellation windows, geographic limitations, vendor.
Variation: "Compare transportation benefits for the plans in scope. Highlight which plans allow rideshare and include the mileage limit per trip."
2
Meals & Grocery Scoped All fields
Can you summarize all meal and grocery benefits available in the selected plans?
Why it works: "all…benefits" signals the AI to extract every sub-field (post-discharge meals, grocery allowance, food wallet, vendor, delivery method) rather than stopping at the first value found. "selected plans" anchors scope.
Key fields extracted: Post-discharge meals count/window/copay, grocery/healthy food allowance, food wallet name, eligible food items, purchase channels, carryover allowed, vendor, exclusions.
Variation: "Summarize all meal and grocery benefits for the plans in scope. Show post-discharge meal details and grocery allowance amounts separately."
3
Dental Scoped All fields Table
Show all dental coverage details for the plans in scope in a tabular format.
Why it works: "all dental coverage details" triggers the AI's full dental extraction schema (9+ fields). "in a tabular format" ensures results are scannable. "plans in scope" is the preferred scope anchor phrase — it maps precisely to the filter state.
Key fields extracted: Covered services, copay/coinsurance, annual maximum, cap structure (combined vs separate), frequency limits, included items, prior auth, vendor/network, exclusions.
Variation: "Show all dental coverage details for the plans in scope. Include the dental financial cap structure (combined vs separate) and prior auth requirements."
4
OTC & Flex Card Discovery Detail drill-down
Which plans offer Flex Card benefits? Please include the benefit details.
Why it works: This two-part pattern — first identify which plans have the benefit, then pull the details — avoids comparing plans where the benefit doesn't exist. The phrase "benefit details" triggers a full OTC/Flex Card field extraction.
Key fields extracted: Card/wallet name, allowance amount, wallet type, buying limits, flex eligible products/services, OTC vendor, network requirement, product list reference.
Tip: Use this "Which plans offer X?" pattern whenever you're unsure which plans have a benefit before comparing. Then follow up with a comparison query.
Variation: "Which plans offer wallet-funded transportation rides? Include the wallet name, funding amount, and carryover rules."
5
Dental Comparison Single field Scoped
Compare the dental exclusions across the selected plans.
Why it works: Targeting a single specific field (exclusions) within a category produces the most accurate, focused response. The AI doesn't need to extract everything — it can concentrate all retrieval effort on the exclusion language sections.
Best for: Deep-dive into one data point across many plans. Use this pattern when you already know which benefit you're looking at and want to compare one dimension.
More single-field examples:
  • "Compare the hearing aid vendor programs across the selected plans."
  • "What are the prior auth requirements for dental across the plans in scope?"
  • "Compare the Telehealth covered language for the plans in scope."
6
Dental Analytical Reasoning
Can you tell me which plan has the strongest dental offering and why?
Why it works: Asking "which plan has the strongest X and why?" prompts the AI to synthesize and rank, not just list. It forces a conclusion with supporting evidence rather than a raw data dump.
Best used: After narrowing to 2–4 plans with filters. With many plans in scope the AI will still answer, but the comparison will be less focused.
Tip: For a fairer ranking, specify the criteria: "Which plan has the strongest dental offering in terms of annual maximum and preventive coverage frequency? Show your reasoning."
Variation: "Which of the selected plans offers the most generous OTC card benefit and why? Compare the allowance amounts and carryover rules."
7
Dental Named fields Scoped Table
Show the preventive and comprehensive dental coverage amounts for the selected plans.
Why it works: Using the exact field names from the EOC document schema ("preventive dental", "comprehensive dental", "coverage amount") dramatically improves retrieval precision. The AI's extraction guide uses these exact terms, so matching them reduces the chance of the AI looking in the wrong section.
Golden rule: When you know the official field name, use it verbatim. Compare:
  • ❌ Less precise: "What dental stuff is covered?"
  • ✅ More precise: "What is the preventive dental financial cap and comprehensive dental financial cap structure for the selected plans?"
Variation: "Show the preventive and comprehensive dental max coverage amounts, and indicate whether the cap structure is combined or separate, for the plans in scope."
8
Drug / Pharmacy Comparison Scoped
Compare the drug benefits offered by the selected plans.
Why it works: Straightforward comparison with a scope anchor. The AI will search for pharmacy/prescription drug sections in the EOC documents.
⚠️ Important caveat: Detailed Part D drug tier and formulary data is generally not embedded in standard EOC supplement documents — it lives in the plan's standalone formulary. If responses are thin, it means the specific drug detail isn't in the indexed EOC files. In that case, ask more broadly:
"What drug coverage or pharmacy benefit language appears in the EOC documents for the selected plans?"
Better alternative: For prescription drug analysis, use the GenieAI chatbot which has PBP-structured data including formulary fields.
9
Hearing & Vision Multi-benefit Scoped Table
Can you summarize the hearing and vision benefits offered by the selected plans and present the information in a table?
Why it works: Combining two related benefits in one query is efficient, and the AI has dedicated extraction schemas for both Hearing and Vision. "Summarize…and present in a table" gives both a scope instruction (extract all) and a format instruction.
Key fields — Hearing: Aid benefit/allowance, premium aids cost share, batteries per aid, battery supply duration, trial period, warranty, vendor, exclusions.
Key fields — Vision: Exam copay, diabetic eye exam copay, allowance/coverage limit, frequency limits, included items (frames, lenses, contacts), vision care provider/vendor, exclusions.
Tip: For deeper accuracy on either, follow up with a dedicated single-benefit query: "Now show me all hearing aid details for the same plans, including battery supply and warranty."
10
OTC & Flex Card Side-by-side Scoped Tabular
Show a side-by-side tabular comparison of OTC benefits for the plans in scope.
Why it works: "Side-by-side tabular" is the clearest format instruction you can give — the AI outputs a table with plans as columns and benefit attributes as rows, which is the easiest format to scan across many plans. "plans in scope" is the cleanest scope anchor.
Key fields extracted: Buying limits, card/wallet name, flex eligible products/services, OTC vendor, allowance amount, network requirement, OTC/Flex covered language, product list reference, wallet type.
Variation: "Show a side-by-side tabular comparison of OTC benefits for the plans in scope. Include allowance amount, wallet type, carryover policy, and OTC vendor."

🧩 Query Design Patterns

Beyond the 10 templates, here are the reusable patterns that make any query more accurate:

Pattern A — Scope + Benefit + Format

The most reliable all-purpose structure:

Template: "Compare/Show/Summarize [benefit + specific fields] for the plans in scope [in a table / side-by-side]."

Examples:
• "Compare the SSBCI/VBID eligibility criteria and wallet structure for the plans in scope in a table."
• "Summarize all telehealth benefits for the selected plans, including eligible modalities and virtual PCP copay, in a table."

Pattern B — Discovery then Detail

Use when you don't know which plans have a benefit:

Step 1: "Which of the selected plans offer [benefit]?"
Step 2: "For those plans, show me the full [benefit] details in a table." (Follow-up in same conversation — the AI remembers the scope.)

Example:
• Step 1: "Which plans offer wallet-funded transportation rides?"
• Step 2: "For those plans, compare the wallet name, funding amount, carryover policy, and rideshare eligibility."

Pattern C — Named Field Drill-Down

Highest accuracy: target a specific field by its official name:

Template: "What is the [exact field name] for the plans in scope?"

High-value field names to use verbatim:
• "dental financial cap structure (combined vs separate)"
• "modality specific costshare rule" (Telehealth)
• "SSBCI/VBID covered language"
• "post-discharge meals window and count"
• "advanced/premium aids cost share" (Hearing)
• "wallet funded rides" (Transportation)
• "OTC/Flex allowance amount detail" + "buying limits"

Pattern D — Analytical Synthesis

For insights, not just data:

Template: "Which plan has the [strongest/most generous/most restrictive] [benefit] and why? Compare [specific criteria]."

Examples:
• "Which plan has the most comprehensive telehealth coverage? Compare eligible modalities and virtual copay rates."
• "Which plan's hearing aid benefit is most generous in terms of allowance and included accessories?"

🔍 Using Filters for Better Accuracy

The filters at the top of the page are the most powerful accuracy lever available — they narrow the document corpus before the AI even starts searching.

  • For Quick Insights: Select 1–4 plans using the filters. With fewer plans, the AI allocates more retrieval capacity per plan and returns richer field-level detail.
  • For free-form queries: Filter to the relevant payor, state, or plan type first. This ensures the AI searches the right subset of documents, not all 898.
  • Plan Name filter: The most targeted option. When you know the exact plan, pin it here before querying — this directs the AI to a single document and maximises the context budget.
  • SNP Type filter: Use this when comparing D-SNP vs non-SNP SSBCI benefits, as eligibility language differs significantly.
⚠️ Scope anchor vs filter: The filters determine which documents are loaded. The phrase "for the plans in scope" in your query tells the AI to use all loaded documents. If you do not apply filters first, "plans in scope" means all 898 plans — and the AI will query only the top 5 by keyword relevance, which may not be the ones you want.

📌 Do's and Don'ts

✅ Do's

  • Always include a scope anchor ("for the selected plans", "for the plans in scope")
  • Specify the output format ("in a table", "side-by-side", "as a comparison")
  • Use exact field names when you know them (e.g. "dental financial cap structure", "modality specific costshare rule")
  • Ask for "all" details when you want comprehensive extraction, not just the headline figure
  • Use 1–4 plan filters before running Quick Insights for highest accuracy
  • Follow up in the same conversation — the AI remembers context and can drill deeper without repeating the scope
  • Ask "which plan has the strongest/most X?" when you want synthesis, not just a data table
  • Request charts with "show as bar chart" / "show as table" to reformat any prior answer

❌ Don'ts

  • Avoid vague queries like "What benefits does this plan have?" — always name the specific benefit category
  • Don't ask about all 898 plans at once without filtering — the AI queries at most 5 documents per response
  • Don't rely on this tool for Part D drug formulary details — that data lives outside EOC documents (use GenieAI)
  • Don't treat AI responses as final without checking the cited source pages — citations are provided for a reason
  • Avoid starting a new conversation for every follow-up — stay in the same thread to retain context and save scope-setting steps
  • Don't combine more than 3–4 benefit categories in a single query — each additional category dilutes retrieval depth
